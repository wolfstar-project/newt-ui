A radio group is for a choice that is short enough to show in full. Where a
[select menu](/docs/components/select-menu) hides its options until asked, this
shows all of them at once — which is what the client's settings panels do when
there are two or three and the difference between them matters.

The state is in the ring and the dot, not in motion: the selected row reads as
selected in a screenshot, and in greyscale.

## Usage

<Usage name="radio" />

The group owns the value; each `Radio` only names the value it stands for. Leave
`value` off the group and it manages its own selection from `defaultValue`.

<div data-framework="react">

```tsx
<RadioGroup label="Input mode" value={mode} onValueChange={setMode}>
  <Radio value="voice-activity">Voice Activity</Radio>
  <Radio value="push-to-talk">Push to Talk</Radio>
</RadioGroup>
```

</div>

<div data-framework="vue">

```vue
<RadioGroup v-model="mode" label="Input mode">
  <Radio value="voice-activity">Voice Activity</Radio>
  <Radio value="push-to-talk">Push to Talk</Radio>
</RadioGroup>
```

</div>

## Second lines

A choice that needs a sentence to explain it takes a `description`, which sits
under the label in muted text. Use it when the label alone would make somebody
guess; leave it off when it would only restate the label.

## Accessibility

The group is `role="radiogroup"` with an accessible name; each row is
`role="radio"` with `aria-checked`. That is the contract a screen reader
announces, and it is why the rows are buttons rather than styled `<input>`
elements — the ring and dot stay stylable without losing the semantics.

The group is **one tab stop**, not one per option: <kbd>Tab</kbd> reaches the
selected row, and <kbd>↑</kbd> <kbd>↓</kbd> <kbd>←</kbd> <kbd>→</kbd> move the
selection within it, wrapping at the ends. <kbd>Home</kbd> and <kbd>End</kbd>
jump to the first and last enabled row. Disabled rows are skipped rather than
focused and refused.

Give the group a `label`. Without one the set is announced with no indication of
what is being chosen, which is the most common way this pattern fails.
