---
title: "Accordion"
description: "Sections that open and close under their headings, one at a time or independently."
---

Use it for content a reader wants one piece of at a time: a short FAQ, the groups of a settings page, the optional details of a form. If every section should be read, plain headings serve better than making the reader open each one.

Every section is a native `<details>` element. The browser keeps one open at a time, opens a closed section when find-in-page matches inside it, and needs no script for any of it.

Both parts take the native attributes of the element they render: a `<div>` for the group and a `<details>` for each section, so a `toggle` listener on an item reports it opening and closing.

## Accessibility

Each title is a `<summary>`, which browsers expose as a button with an expanded state; `Enter` and `Space` toggle it and `Tab` moves between titles. Write a title that makes sense alone, because a screen reader user moves through the titles before opening any. The chevron is decoration, turns a quarter when the section is open, and is mirrored in right-to-left text.
